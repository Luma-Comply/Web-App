<article>

<p>Your practice is spending roughly <strong>$68,274 per year</strong> on prior authorization administration. That's the average, according to the American Medical Association's 2023 survey data. If you're running a specialty practice that leans heavily on biologics — rheumatology, dermatology, oncology, gastroenterology — the real number is almost certainly higher.</p>

<p>Most practice managers know PA is painful. Few have actually done the math on what it costs. Let's do it together.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>The Core Calculation: Staff Time Is Where It Bleeds</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.caqh.org/insights/caqh-index" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CAQH Index</a> pegs the average manual PA transaction at <strong>20–45 minutes of staff time</strong>. That range matters — a straightforward commercial plan PA might take 20 minutes, but a biologic PA for a specialty drug with step-therapy requirements and peer-to-peer documentation? Closer to 45.</p>

<p>Here's a simple framework to run your own numbers:</p>

<blockquote>
<strong>Monthly PA volume</strong> × <strong>avg. time per case (hours)</strong> × <strong>loaded hourly rate</strong> = monthly staff cost
</blockquote>

<p>Example: A mid-size rheumatology practice processing 80 PAs per month, at 40 minutes each, with a billing coordinator earning $22/hour (loaded to ~$30 with benefits):</p>

<ul>
  <li>80 cases × 0.67 hours = 53.6 hours/month</li>
  <li>53.6 hours × $30/hour = <strong>$1,608/month</strong></li>
  <li>Annualized: <strong>$19,296 in staff time alone</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>That's just the baseline. It doesn't account for denials, rework, or the cases that slip through the cracks.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Denials Are Where the Real Money Disappears</h2>

<p><a href="https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/prior-authorization-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AMA data shows</a> that 35% of physicians report waiting 3 or more business days for PA decisions — and roughly one in five requests gets denied on the first submission. For biologics, denial rates on initial submission run even higher, often 30–40% depending on the payer.</p>

<p>Each denial triggers a rework cycle. Appeals require pulling clinical notes, gathering additional documentation, often scheduling a peer-to-peer call. Add another 60–90 minutes per denial case.</p>

<p>Back to our 80-case-per-month practice: if 20% get denied (16 cases), and each rework takes 75 minutes:</p>

<ul>
  <li>16 cases × 1.25 hours × $30/hour = <strong>$600/month in denial rework</strong></li>
  <li>Annualized: <strong>$7,200 just to fight denials</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>Some of those appeals still fail. Every failed appeal on a biologic claim can mean $3,000–$15,000 in lost revenue depending on the drug and indication.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Opportunity Cost: The Revenue Sitting on the Table</h2>

<p>Here's the number most practices undercount: while your coordinator is on hold with a payer's PA line, they're not doing anything else. Each hour spent on manual PA is an hour not spent on eligibility checks, claim follow-up, or scheduling.</p>

<p>At 53.6 hours/month of PA work, that's about 13 hours per week of a coordinator's time — effectively a half-time position dedicated to one administrative function.</p>

<p>The physician's time is even more expensive. Peer-to-peer calls average 20 minutes each, and they typically require the physician directly. At a conservative $200/hour physician cost, 10 peer-to-peers per month costs <strong>$667/month</strong> in physician time — <strong>$8,000/year</strong> — for calls that often get denied anyway.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Revenue Delay Has a Real Dollar Value</h2>

<p>When a PA takes 5–10 business days and then gets denied, you've potentially delayed treatment by 2–3 weeks. For practices billing on a per-infusion or per-injection basis, that's 2–3 revenue cycles gone.</p>

<p>Patient abandonment compounds this. The <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8653000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">research on specialty drug abandonment</a> is consistent: when PA delays stretch past 2 weeks, 10–20% of patients give up on the therapy. For a practice administering 15 biologic infusions per month at $1,500 revenue per visit, losing even one patient per month to abandonment is <strong>$18,000/year in lost revenue</strong>.</p>

<p>Add up the components for our example practice and you're already past $50,000 annually before you've factored in overhead.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>The Hidden Costs Nobody Talks About</h2>

<p>Staff turnover in billing and prior auth roles runs high — and for good reason. It's demoralizing work: repetitive, high-friction, and the outcomes are often outside your control. The <a href="https://www.mgma.com/data/benchmarking-resources/mgma-staffing-benchmarking-reports" target="_blank" rel="noopener">MGMA estimates</a> that replacing a medical billing specialist costs $8,000–$12,000 when you factor in recruiting, onboarding, and the 3–6 months before they're fully productive.</p>

<p>Physician burnout is harder to quantify but impossible to ignore. Surveys consistently rank PA among the top contributors to administrative burden. When senior clinicians spend 20% of their week on documentation and appeals instead of patients, retention suffers — and replacing a physician costs 1–2x their annual salary.</p>

<p>There's also the downstream clinical impact. Delayed biologics for conditions like severe rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's disease, or moderate-to-severe psoriasis aren't just inconvenient. Disease progression during coverage gaps leads to worse outcomes, more urgent care visits, and the kind of patient experience that drives negative reviews and referral losses.</p>

<h2>Run Your Own Numbers</h2>

<p>The framework is simple. Take your monthly PA volume, multiply by your average time per case, multiply by your loaded staff rate. Add 20% for denial rework. Add your physician's hourly rate times peer-to-peer frequency. Multiply by 12.</p>

<p>If the number surprises you, you're not alone. Most practice managers have never seen it laid out this way — because the costs are distributed across multiple line items and rarely aggregated.</p>

<p>The good news: this is one of the more solvable problems in medical administration. Unlike payer reimbursement rates or regulatory compliance, PA efficiency is largely within your control. The technology to automate the documentation and tracking layer exists today, it's HIPAA-compliant, and the <a href="https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2021.01021" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ROI math works</a> at almost any practice size once you've done the calculation above.</p>

<p>Start with the math. The decision usually gets pretty easy from there.</p>
