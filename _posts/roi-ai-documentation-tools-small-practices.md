<article>

<p>Large health systems have implementation teams, enterprise contracts, and tolerance for 18-month ROI timelines. A 10-physician rheumatology practice does not. If you're running a small or mid-size practice, the technology evaluation question is simpler and harsher: does this pay for itself, and how quickly?</p>

<p>Let's work through the actual math. No filler — just the numbers.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>The Baseline: What Prior Auth Documentation Actually Costs You</h2>

<p>For a 10-provider rheumatology practice, biologics prior authorization is a significant operational load. Based on <a href="https://www.caqh.org/insights/caqh-index" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CAQH Index data</a>, biologic PAs average <strong>30–45 minutes of staff time each</strong> for documentation preparation — pulling clinical notes, formatting against payer criteria, ensuring step therapy documentation is complete.</p>

<p>A typical 10-physician rheumatology practice processes roughly <strong>80–120 biologic PAs per month</strong>. Let's use 100 as our working number.</p>

<p>At 35 minutes per case (middle of the range) with a billing coordinator at a loaded rate of $30/hour (salary + benefits):</p>

<ul>
  <li>100 cases × 0.58 hours = <strong>58 hours/month</strong> on documentation</li>
  <li>58 hours × $30/hour = <strong>$1,740/month</strong></li>
  <li>Annualized: <strong>$20,880/year in staff time</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>That's just documentation prep. It doesn't include phone time, fax follow-ups, or denial rework.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Adding Denials: Where the Real Money Leaks</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/prior-authorization/prior-authorization-overview" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AMA reports</a> that roughly 25% of biologic PA requests get denied on first submission across commercial payers — and for some payers, that number runs higher. At 25%, our 100-case practice is processing 25 denials per month.</p>

<p>Each denial triggers appeals work. The appeal process for a biologic typically runs 60–90 minutes of staff time plus, frequently, a physician peer-to-peer call.</p>

<ul>
  <li>25 denials × 75 min rework = 31.25 hours of staff time</li>
  <li>31.25 hours × $30 = <strong>$937.50/month in denial rework</strong></li>
  <li>10 peer-to-peer calls (40% of denials escalate) × 20 min physician time × $200/hr = <strong>$667/month in physician time</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>Monthly denial cost: <strong>$1,604</strong>. Annualized: <strong>$19,254.</strong></p>

<p>Running total: $20,880 + $19,254 = <strong>$40,134/year in PA documentation costs</strong> before patient impact.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>Patient Abandonment: The Revenue Loss Most Practices Don't Track</h2>

<p>Biologic PAs that drag past 2 weeks have a documented abandonment problem. <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8653000/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Published research</a> consistently shows 10–20% of specialty drug patients abandon therapy when prior auth delays extend beyond 2 weeks. For a practice administering biologic infusions or injections, that's direct revenue.</p>

<p>Conservative assumption: 2 patients per month abandon or delay therapy due to PA friction. At $1,500 average revenue per biologic infusion visit:</p>

<ul>
  <li>2 patients × $1,500 × 12 months = <strong>$36,000/year in lost or delayed revenue</strong></li>
</ul>

<p>This is a rough number — it depends on your specific payer mix, biologics administered, and how your practice bills. But even at half this estimate, it's material.</p>

<h2>The AI Tool Alternative: Running the Numbers</h2>

<p>AI documentation tools for prior authorization compress the per-case documentation time from 30–45 minutes to 2–5 minutes. The tool pulls structured clinical data, applies it against payer-specific criteria, and generates the medical necessity letter. Staff reviews and submits rather than building from scratch.</p>

<p>What changes with AI-assisted documentation:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Documentation time:</strong> 100 cases × 4 min = 6.7 hours/month (vs. 58 hours) → saves 51.3 hours/month</li>
  <li><strong>51.3 hours × $30/hour = $1,539/month saved</strong> in documentation staff time alone</li>
  <li><strong>Denial rate improvement:</strong> Better payer-aligned documentation typically reduces first-pass denials by 30–50%. At 30% improvement, denials drop from 25 to 17–18 per month. Denial rework savings: ~$280/month</li>
  <li><strong>Patient abandonment:</strong> Faster approvals reduce the window for abandonment. Conservative estimate: 1 fewer abandonment per month = $1,500/month recovered</li>
</ul>

<p>Combined monthly savings: approximately <strong>$3,319/month</strong>.</p>

<h2>The Soft ROI: Physician Satisfaction and Patient Retention</h2>

<p>Two benefits that don't appear in the spreadsheet but matter for small practice sustainability:</p>

<p><strong>Physician satisfaction.</strong> Peer-to-peer calls are a documented source of physician frustration — not because they're long, but because they're often avoidable. When documentation quality on initial submission improves, the call volume drops. Fewer peer-to-peers per month is a measurable quality-of-work improvement for physicians. In specialties where burnout rates run 45–55%, removing friction from the highest-frustration workflows has real retention value. Replacing a departing physician costs <strong>$500,000 to $1 million</strong> all-in — recruiting, credentialing, panel rebuilding, and the revenue gap during transition.</p>

<p><strong>Patient retention.</strong> Patients who experience repeated biologic delays leave. They don't always say why — they just don't rebook, or they transfer to a practice they found on ZocDoc. Faster approvals reduce the friction that leads to quiet attrition. For a practice that depends on recurring biologic infusion revenue, losing one established patient is a $10,000–$30,000 lifetime value hit depending on drug and condition.</p>

<hr/>

<h2>What the Math Actually Tells You</h2>

<p>For a 10-provider practice running 80–120 biologic PAs per month, the ROI on AI documentation tooling is not a close call. The tool costs less than what you're spending on a single week of PA documentation staff time.</p>

<p>The harder question is implementation: will your staff actually use it, and will it integrate with how you submit PAs today? That's the evaluation work that matters — not whether the ROI is positive (it is), but whether the workflow fit is good enough to capture it.</p>

<p>A practical starting point: track your actual PA documentation time for one month. Not what you think it is — what it actually is, in time logs. The number is almost always higher than the estimate. That measurement makes the decision significantly easier.</p>
